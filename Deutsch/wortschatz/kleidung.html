📖 Grammar & Article Guide

der — Maskulinum (Masculine)

Many clothing nouns are masculine, especially compounds ending in -schuh, -mantel, -anzug, -hut, or -gürtel.

Ich trage den Pullover. (I'm wearing the sweater.)

die — Femininum (Feminine)

Feminine clothing nouns often end in -e (Hose, Jacke, Bluse, Mütze, Socke). All plural forms are also die.

Ich ziehe die Jacke an. (I put on the jacket.)

das — Neutrum (Neuter)

Short native words (Hemd, Kleid) and many English loanwords (T-Shirt) default to neuter.

Das Kleid ist wunderschön. (The dress is beautiful.)

🔗 Compound Word Rule

The last part of a compound decides the gender. Regen + der Mantel → der Regenmantel. Bade + die Hose → die Badehose.

Sonnen + die Brille = die Sonnenbrille (sunglasses)

💡 Common Mistake

anziehen ("to put on") is a separable verb — the prefix jumps to the end of the sentence. Don't say "Ich anziehe die Jacke."

Correct: Ich ziehe die Schuhe an. (I put on the shoes.)

📝 Exam Tips

Items German treats as one grammatically singular piece — like die Hose (pants) or die Brille (glasses) — are singular even though English uses a plural noun.

die Hose ist blau (singular!) — not "the pants are blue."

🧠 Memory Trick

Use color-coding: Blue = der, Pink = die, Gold = das. Try to picture the word in its color when memorizing.

der (🔵 blue) Mantel, die (🩷 pink) Jacke, das (🟡 gold) Hemd
