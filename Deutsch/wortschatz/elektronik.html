📖 Grammar & Article Guide

der — Maskulinum (Masculine)

Many device names ending in -er (agent nouns describing "the thing that does X") are masculine.

Ich schalte den Drucker ein. (I switch on the printer.)

die — Femininum (Feminine)

Used for feminine nouns and ALL plural forms. The plural article is always die.

Ich schalte die Waschmaschine ein. (I switch on the washing machine.)

das — Neutrum (Neuter)

Used for neuter nouns. Many short English tech loanwords default to das.

Das Handy ist leer. (The phone battery is dead.)

🔗 Compound Word Rule

German compound nouns take the gender of their last component. Wasch + Maschine → die Waschmaschine, because die Maschine is feminine.

Kopf + der Hörer = der Kopfhörer (headphones)

💡 Common Mistake

Don't guess a loanword's gender from its English meaning — it's often unpredictable. Always learn the noun with its article!

Say: "das Handy" — never "der Handy," even though it looks masculine.

📝 Exam Tips

Device nouns ending in -er are usually der. Nouns ending in -e are usually die. The plural is always die, no matter the singular article.

die Fernbedienungen (the remote controls) — always die in plural!

🧠 Memory Trick

Use color-coding: Blue = der, Pink = die, Gold = das. Try to picture the word in its color when memorizing.

der (🔵 blue) Computer, die (🩷 pink) Kamera, das (🟡 gold) Handy
