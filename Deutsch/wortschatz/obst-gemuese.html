📖 Grammar & Article Guide

der — Maskulinum (Masculine)

A minority of fruit and vegetable names are masculine — usually plain, short nouns with no special ending.

Ich esse den Apfel. (I eat the apple.)

die — Femininum (Feminine)

Most German fruit and vegetable names are feminine — a handy rule of thumb when guessing! All plural forms are also die.

Ich kaufe die Tomate. (I buy the tomato.)

das — Neutrum (Neuter)

Rare among individual items, but diminutives ending in -chen are always neuter — and the collective words das Obst (fruit) and das Gemüse (vegetables) are neuter too.

Das Radieschen ist scharf. (The radish is spicy.)

🔗 A Useful Pattern

Around 80% of the words in this lesson are die — so when you meet a new fruit or vegetable and aren't sure, "die" is statistically your best guess. Always confirm with a dictionary though!

die Mango, die Ananas, die Zucchini — all loanwords, all die.

💡 Common Mistake

Don't assume a loanword takes "der" just because it sounds foreign or ends in a consonant. Always learn the noun with its article!

Say: "die Ananas" — never "der Ananas."

📝 Exam Tips

Compound produce names take the gender of their last part: Wasser + die Melone → die Wassermelone. The plural is always die, whatever the singular article.

die Kartoffeln (the potatoes) — always die in plural!

🧠 Memory Trick

Use color-coding: Blue = der, Pink = die, Gold = das. Try to picture the word in its color when memorizing.

der (🔵 blue) Kürbis, die (🩷 pink) Tomate, das (🟡 gold) Radieschen
